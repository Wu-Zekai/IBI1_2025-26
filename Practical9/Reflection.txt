This week's practical is supper hard and challenging.

What I found most challenging was transitioning from the "well-mixed" population in the simple SIR model to the 2D Spatial SIR model. Specifically, managing the 8-neighbor indexing while ensuring the simulation didn't crash at the boundaries of the 100x100 grid required careful logical thinking. I also learned the importance of using .copy() when updating the population state to avoid simultaneous update errors, where new infections could spread prematurely within the same time step.

One of the highlights was the vaccination simulation. It was highly satisfying to see the "herd immunity" effect visualized through color-mapped curves. It made me realize how a stochastic model can capture the randomness of real-life outbreaks much more realistically than a purely deterministic formula.

The most important takeaway for me was mastering the use of NumPy's 'where' and 'random.choice' functions to handle large-scale probabilistic events efficiently. This practical has significantly boosted my confidence in building complex, multi-dimensional simulations in Python.
