Question: 
How has the relationship between the DALY rates in China and the United Kingdom 
changed over time between 1990 and 2019? Are they becoming more similar?

Code Location: 
The code to answer this question starts at the bottom of dalys.py, under the 
section "Task 6: Self-Question Analysis".

Discussion:
1. Result: In 1990, China had a significantly higher DALY rate (around 38,000) 
   compared to the UK (around 25,000). However, China's DALY rate has shown a 
   sharp and consistent decline over the 30-year period.
2. Similarity: By 2019, the gap between the two countries has narrowed 
   substantially. The data shows they are becoming much more similar.
3. Reasons: This trend aligns with expectations regarding global health transitions. 
   China's rapid economic growth has led to significant improvements in public health 
   infrastructure, better sanitation, and broader access to vaccination and 
   healthcare services. This has greatly reduced the burden of infectious diseases 
   and neonatal conditions. In contrast, the UK, as a long-developed nation, 
   started from a lower baseline and primarily deals with non-communicable 
   diseases associated with an aging population, leading to a slower rate of 
   decline in DALYs compared to a rapidly developing nation like China.
